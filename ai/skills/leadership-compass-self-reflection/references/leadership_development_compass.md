# Leadership Development Compass  - Engineering Managers

**Author:** Stefan, Director of Engineering
**Date:** February 2026

---
## Framework Introduction


This is a **leadership development framework**, not a performance measurement tool. It will not be used to determine ratings or evaluate your performance in isolation. It is designed to help us understand your natural leadership patterns and create development plans that build on your strengths.
This framework is adapted from a director-level version I created, drawing on ideas from Marcus Buckingham's *First, Break All The Rules*, Patrick Lencioni's *The Advantage*, Will Larson's *An Elegant Puzzle*, and Kim Scott's *Radical Candor*, along with my own observations about what makes engineering managers effective in our environment. It follows the established **people, process, technology** principle of leadership  - you don't have to be great at all three, but the lower you are on one, the better you need to be at the other two.



### The Core Principle


I believe there isn't one model that an Engineering Manager should follow. Rather, it's about each EM developing their own leadership style that maximizes their natural talents and manages around their limitations  - while ensuring that as a leadership team, our collective capabilities cover what the organization needs.
Each dimension is assessed on a sliding scale from **1–10**, but the score determines your **development strategy**, not your value as a leader.
---


## The Three Action Categories


### Maximize  - Scores 7–10
**Definition:** Areas where your natural thinking patterns, instincts, and energy align perfectly with the demands of the role. These feel effortless and energizing to you.
**Observable Signs:**
- You naturally gravitate toward these activities
- You learn quickly in these areas with minimal instruction
- You generate creative solutions others don't see
- You feel energized, not drained, after engaging in these activities
- Others frequently seek you out for help in these areas
**Development Strategy:**
- Maximize and leverage  - these are your superpowers
- Create opportunities to use these capabilities more frequently
- Apply these talents across broader areas and teams
- Use these areas to compensate for weaker areas on the team

### Develop  - Scores 4–6
**Definition:** Areas where focused development will yield strong returns. With targeted effort, you'll continue to improve.
**Observable Signs:**
- You show interest and engagement when learning about these areas
- With coaching or training, you demonstrate measurable improvement
- You can perform adequately in these areas when you focus attention
- You don't actively avoid these responsibilities
- Small improvements yield noticeable performance gains
**Development Strategy:**
- Invest through coaching, reading, identifying opportunities, reflecting, inspecting and adapting
- Set specific, measurable goals for improvement
- Create low-risk practice opportunities

### Manage Around  - Scores 1–3
**Definition:** Areas where your natural wiring makes progress more challenging and less energizing. Your energy and effort will typically yield better returns when focused elsewhere.
**Observable Signs:**
- You know you won't excel here, even with lots of effort and training
- These activities drain your energy and enthusiasm
- You avoid these responsibilities when possible
- Improvement requires disproportionate effort for minimal results
**Development Strategy:**
- Manage around through partnerships and systems  - don't try to fix
- Create support structures (strong Tech Leads, processes, tools)
- Partner with others who excel in these areas
- Design your role to minimize time spent in these areas


### Remember


The goal isn't to be 8+ in everything. The goal is to:
- Have **2–3 dimensions to maximize** (7+)
- **Develop** other areas to competency (4–6)
- Know how to **manage around** limitations (1–3)
The scores represent where your natural abilities lie and suggest where to focus your energy for greatest impact. However, **Team Leadership Contribution** and **Collaboration & Cross-Team Influence** require a minimum level of capability to operate successfully as an Engineering Manager  - these are fundamental to the role.
---


## Living with the Framework


This framework is meant to be a living tool, not an annual exercise. Here's how to engage with it:
**Quarterly Reflection:**
- Self-assess your current level and growth trajectory in each dimension
- Ask: "What evidence shows I've grown this quarter?"
- Document specific situations that revealed your capability level
- Review which development strategies you've actively pursued
**Growth Progression:** Moving up a level means specific, observable changes. For example, going from 5 to 6 in People Leadership means your Tech Leads operate with greater autonomy, needing your input only on edge cases rather than regular decisions.
**Using the Framework in Practice:**
- Reference it when taking on new initiatives  - which dimensions will be critical?
- Use it in 1:1s with your manager to discuss development priorities
- Revisit after major challenges to understand what dimensions were tested

The Six Dimensions


### 1. People Leadership Effectiveness
*Building and energizing high-performing teams*
**Definition:** The ability to inspire, develop, and engage your engineering team(s) while creating psychological safety and driving performance beyond expectations.
**What This Looks Like at EM Level:**
- Your team is energized by interactions with you, not drained
- Engineers grow and develop under your leadership
- Underperformers either improve significantly or are addressed appropriately
- Tech Leads you develop can operate with increasing independence
- Your team proactively surfaces problems and ideas
- Psychological safety enables learning from failure and calculated risk-taking
**Observable Indicators:**
- Team retention and engagement
- Engineer growth and skill development
- Tech Lead autonomy and effectiveness
- Frequency and quality of problem escalation
- Team willingness to experiment and take ownership
**Business Impact When Strong:**
- Faster delivery through stable, engaged teams
- Reduced incidents from retained expertise
- Lower hiring and onboarding costs from improved retention
- Accelerated learning and innovation from psychological safety
**Development Strategies:**
- **Maximize (7–10):** Lead team culture initiatives, mentor other EMs, drive engagement practices across the domain
- **Develop (4–6):** Leadership coaching, 1:1 effectiveness training, feedback frameworks, mentoring programs
- **Manage Around (1–3):** Hire strong Tech Leads, create systematic people processes, partner with people-oriented peers
---

### 2. Process Excellence and Systems Thinking
*Creating scalable ways of working*
**Definition:** Designing and implementing sustainable, repeatable processes that enable predictable delivery and continuous improvement within your team(s).
**What This Looks Like at EM Level:**
- Your team's processes function effectively without your constant involvement
- You think in workflows and systems, not individual fixes
- Processes reduce friction rather than create bureaucracy
- Bottlenecks are anticipated and addressed before they escalate
- Continuous improvement is part of how your team works
**Observable Indicators:**
- Process adoption and sustainability within your team(s)
- Reduction in operational surprises
- Predictability of delivery and sprint outcomes
- Time from identifying a problem to implementing a systematic solution
- Whether processes survive your absence
**Business Impact When Strong:**
- Higher deployment frequency and faster time-to-market
- Lower mean time to recovery and reduced operational costs
- Predictable delivery enables accurate business commitments
- Scalable team growth without proportional chaos
**Development Strategies:**
- **Maximize (7–10):** Lead process improvements across the domain, design frameworks others can adopt
- **Develop (4–6):** Deeper learning on lean/agile principles, study established engineering process frameworks, experiment with lightweight improvements
- **Manage Around (1–3):** Partner with process-oriented peers or Tech Leads, implement pre-built frameworks rather than designing from scratch
---

### 3. Technical Engagement and Judgment
*Guiding sound technical decisions*
**Definition:** Contributing meaningfully to technical discussions, ensuring sound technical direction within your team(s), and translating between technical depth and business impact.
**What This Looks Like at EM Level:**
- You contribute valuable perspective to technical discussions (operational, cost, delivery impact)
- Engineers respect your technical input even if you're not the deepest expert
- You understand and can articulate technical trade-offs to stakeholders
- You ensure technical decisions consider real-world constraints (timelines, resources, customer impact)
- You ask the right questions in architecture and design discussions
- You partner effectively with Tech Leads and architects on technical direction
**Observable Indicators:**
- Quality of your participation in technical discussions and design reviews
- Engineer confidence in your technical judgment
- Ability to translate technical decisions into business language for stakeholders
- Balance between innovation and stability in your team's technical choices
- Frequency of customer and product impact considerations in technical decisions
**Business Impact When Strong:**
- Reduced technical debt through informed trade-off decisions
- Faster product delivery enabled by pragmatic technical choices
- Improved system reliability affecting customer retention
- Better resource allocation through understanding of technical effort
**Development Strategies:**
- **Maximize (7–10):** Lead technical direction conversations, mentor others in translating technical concepts for business audiences
- **Develop (4–6):** Participate more actively in design reviews, pair with Tech Leads on architectural trade-offs, build enough depth to ask the right questions
- **Manage Around (1–3):** Partner closely with strong Tech Leads and architects, focus on the operational and business lens you bring, ensure technical leaders have clear space to lead
---

### 4. Team Leadership Contribution
*Operating as part of the leadership system*
**Definition:** Functioning as an effective member of the broader leadership team, balancing your team's needs with domain and organizational priorities.
**What This Looks Like at EM Level:**
- You actively engage with challenges beyond your immediate team
- You balance team advocacy with domain and organizational needs
- You reliably deliver on commitments made to your manager and peers
- You share information transparently and proactively
- You think "we" not "my team" for domain-level issues
- You support domain initiatives even when they create short-term challenges for your team
**Observable Indicators:**
- Reliability of follow-through on commitments to peers and leadership
- Quality and timeliness of information sharing (up, down, and sideways)
- Engagement in domain-level discussions and initiatives
- Balance between team protection and organizational progress
- Peer assessment of collaboration effectiveness
**Business Impact When Strong:**
- Faster resolution of cross-team dependencies and blockers
- Aligned execution across the domain
- Reduced duplicate effort and miscommunication
- Better risk management through shared ownership of challenges
**Development Strategies:**
- **Maximize (7–10):** Lead domain-level initiatives, model collaborative behavior, mentor other EMs
- **Develop (4–6):** Practice proactive information sharing, engage in cross-team initiatives, seek feedback on collaboration effectiveness
- **Manage Around (1–3):** ⚠️ This is a critical area for EM-level effectiveness  - leadership team contribution is fundamental to the role. If this is a consistent 1–3, it must be addressed directly.
---

### 5. Clarity Creation and Communication
*Making the complex understandable*
**Definition:** Translating complexity into clarity, ensuring your team and stakeholders understand the what, why, and how of work and decisions.
**What This Looks Like at EM Level:**
- Your team maintains clarity even during ambiguous or changing circumstances
- You communicate consistently and effectively across diverse audiences (engineers, product, design, leadership)
- Complex technical concepts become accessible to non-technical stakeholders
- Upward communication to your manager is proactive and transparent
- Your team can articulate why their work matters and how it connects to business goals
- Information flows quickly and accurately in all directions
**Observable Indicators:**
- Team clarity and alignment during periods of change
- Stakeholder understanding and confidence
- Speed and accuracy of information flow within and beyond your team
- Quality of written and verbal communications
- Frequency of communication-based misunderstandings or surprises
**Business Impact When Strong:**
- Reduced rework from clear requirements and expectations
- Faster decision-making through effective information flow
- Improved stakeholder confidence and trust
- Lower resistance to change through clear communication
- Better cross-functional relationships through transparency
**Development Strategies:**
- **Maximize (7–10):** Lead complex stakeholder conversations, mentor others in communication, create frameworks your team and peers can use
- **Develop (4–6):** Practice communicating to diverse audiences, seek feedback on clarity, study communication frameworks, lean into written formats for important decisions
- **Manage Around (1–3):** Partner with excellent communicators for key stakeholder interactions, create templates and pre-read documents, use written async formats where verbal communication is less effective
---


### 6. Collaboration and Cross-Team Influence
*Driving outcomes beyond your immediate team*
**Definition:** Creating value beyond your immediate responsibility through influence, collaboration, and relationship-building across teams and functions.
**What This Looks Like at EM Level:**
- Other teams actively seek your input and partnership
- You build productive relationships with Product, Design, and other engineering teams
- You influence through expertise and relationship, not just authority
- You contribute to initiatives that benefit the broader domain, not just your team
- You follow through on commitments to other teams with the same priority as internal ones
- You advocate for your team's needs while remaining open to organizational trade-offs
**Observable Indicators:**
- Frequency of collaboration requests from other teams
- Quality of relationships with Product Managers, Designers, and peer EMs
- Follow-through on cross-team commitments and dependencies
- Contributions to domain-level or organization-level initiatives
- Reputation and trust beyond your immediate team
**Business Impact When Strong:**
- Successful delivery of cross-team initiatives
- Reduced organizational friction and coordination costs
- Faster product integration and delivery across teams
- Better resource allocation through influence on priorities
- Stronger organizational culture through collaborative leadership
**Development Strategies:**
- **Maximize (7–10):** Lead cross-team initiatives, build domain-wide partnerships, contribute to culture and practices beyond your team
- **Develop (4–6):** Actively seek cross-team collaboration opportunities, build relationships with peer EMs and stakeholders, practice stakeholder engagement
- **Manage Around (1–3):** ⚠️ This is a critical area for EM-level effectiveness  - cross-team influence is fundamental to the role. If this is a consistent 1–3, it must be addressed directly.

## How to Use This Document
1. **Self-assess** each dimension honestly on the 1–10 scale
2. **Identify your action category** for each (Maximize, Develop, or Manage Around)
3. **Bring your self-assessment to your next 1:1**  - we'll compare perspectives and align on priorities
4. **Pick 1–2 Develop areas** to focus on each quarter, alongside continuing to maximize your strengths
5. **Revisit quarterly**  - track evidence of growth and adjust strategies
This is a conversation tool. The value isn't in the scores  - it's in the honest discussion about where you naturally excel, where you can grow, and how we build a leadership team that covers all the dimensions together.
